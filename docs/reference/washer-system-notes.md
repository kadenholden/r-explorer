# Screen-wash system — researched notes (2026-08-15)

Gathered because the operator's car has a washer leak and it's the first
job on the list. Sources at the bottom; anything not nailed down is
flagged rather than guessed.

## Architecture — it's TWO tanks

> "The factory washer reservoir on the Volkswagen MK7 Golf R and Audi 8V
> S3 is made up of two parts, an upper and a lower reservoir." The lower
> "houses the majority of the washer fluid capacity" while the upper
> "acts as a filler neck." — Spulen/USP relocation-kit listing

So the blue cap in the engine bay sits **directly on top of the upper
tank** — you pour straight into a tank, not down a long pipe. Factory
capacity quoted as **3.7 L** for the R/S3. VW's owner's manual gives
"approximately 3–4 litres, and approximately 3–6 litres in vehicles with
a headlight washer system."

## Pumps — this car has TWO

- **V59** — windscreen **and rear screen** washer pump (one pump, two
  colour-coded outlets).
- **V11** — headlight washer pump, **high pressure**, fitted only to cars
  with headlamp washing. It sits "on the outside of the reservoir closest
  to the fender", i.e. opposite the windscreen pump.
- Standard (no headlamp washer) cars get a smaller tank and one pump.

This car has bi-xenon headlights (R10), so it has headlamp washers —
which are also an **MOT requirement** on xenon cars.

- **Level sender** lights the low-fluid warning and sits just above the
  windscreen pump. G33 is the standard VAG designation for this sender;
  the exact part is UNVERIFIED against ETKA.

## Access — arch liner, not the bumper

VW's own procedure for removing the **whole reservoir** is heavy: remove
the front bumper cover, remove a headlight, "if necessary, remove
headlight washer system pump -V11-", then release the filler neck from
its bracket on the lock carrier and unbolt the tank.

For **pumps, grommets and the level sender**, owners go in through the
front wheel-arch liner instead: wheel off, T25 Torx liner fixings out,
liner folded back — the pump sits at the bottom of the tank with the
level sender above it. Under an hour.

Side: the operator's own photos put the filler on the **offside (right)**
of this RHD car; the US guide describes the reservoir behind the
"passenger-side" (i.e. right-hand) arch liner, which agrees. One VW
manual page says "remove left headlight", which conflicts — that page's
model generation is unclear, so **the photos win**.

## Leak points, in the order worth checking

1. **Pump grommet** — the rubber ring the pump pushes into. Hardens with
   age and weeps; the number-one cause of a bottle that empties itself
   while parked. Pennies to replace.
2. **Level-sender seal** — same story, smaller.
3. **Tank cracks** — stone strikes, or freezing when filled with plain
   water. Often only weeps when full.
4. **Upper-to-lower throat joint** — leaks only when brim-full, so the
   leak appears intermittent.
5. **Hoses/jets** — only wet when the washers are actually used. A
   headlamp jet stuck open dribbles and mimics a tank leak.

**Fitting trap (both pumps):** do not push the grommet into the tank
while refitting — it cannot be retrieved without dropping the whole
reservoir. Wet it with washer fluid and press the pump in gently.
